## Core Principles

These are pulled directly from her portfolio and reflect how she actually makes product decisions:

### 1. Signal over noise
> *"The most valuable data is rarely where it's convenient to look. I go where it's messy first because that's usually where the real problem is hiding."*

This shows up in practice: she extracted research application classification from four years of free-text CRM notes instead of starting fresh with a clean new field — because the historical signal was only in the mess.

### 2. Build to test, not to spec
> *"A working prototype surfaces more truth than a thorough document. I put something real in front of users before defending a plan."*

After experiencing a 3.5-week delay waiting for an engineer to surface a Figma handoff in a browser, she learned to build working React prototypes herself (via AI-assisted development) so she could test interactions in real time and arrive at user conversations with something concrete.

### 3. Domain fluency is the design brief
> *"You can't design guardrails for a system you don't understand. I make it a practice to learn the domain before touching the interface."*

For PolyTrace, she worked with an in-house neuroscientist to map every CaImAn parameter to a plain-language user need before designing any UI. She treats domain learning as a prerequisite, not background reading.

### 4. Move the right number
> *"A metric that moves without moving the business is noise with better formatting. I define what 'right' means before building anything."*

Her statistics background sets a high bar: the six-tab V1 dashboard she built on data availability assumptions got cut to three tabs (Revenue, Pipeline, Performance) after one conversation with the actual user — because each tab needed to map to a real quarterly review decision, not a data category.
